THE GILDED CIRCUIT — FIRST PLAYABLE EXPEDITION
Target first playthrough: 10–15 minutes. This is a design target, not a measured playtime.

THE HOOK
An elevated aqueduct circles a golden bell tower. Couriers stream around it, bells ring and the town celebrates record output. Below, a kitchen waits beside a dock full of undelivered supplies. Make the contradiction observable within the first minute.

MAP AND CAMERA
One small connected location: arrival dock / waiting kitchen → accessible path beneath the circuit → observation balcony → route-and-reward station → return dock. A short optional overlook supplies wonder and a quiet companion moment. No long empty walk or maze of menus.
Begin with shallow 2.5D or side-on movement that gives the concept plate depth while keeping paths clear. Reuse the old isometric camera if a quick playable comparison is materially better. Pick one camera before final asset production. The concept illustration is a mood/composition reference, not a collision map.

BEATS
1. Arrival, 0–2 minutes: immediate movement, Pip responds, a waiting resident establishes the concrete need. See at least one courier return carrying the same crate.
2. Observation, 2–4: follow a courier around the bell loop. Inspect actual cargo and the celebratory tally. Pip can watch a route you are not standing beside.
3. Experiment, 4–8: use physical controls to change one reward or temporarily silence a bell. Run a short trial; compare real deliveries with the score. Undo and retry without losing your discovery notes.
4. Consequence, 8–11: couriers choose the service route and unload visible crates. The kitchen lights, people gather and a path opens naturally through a now-served departure dock. The world acknowledges what actually happened.
5. Return, 11–15: brief warm scene, optional research note, a new expedition invitation. End with curiosity rather than a giant scorecard.
Timings are pacing targets. Do not wait on artificial timers to force the duration.

SMALL, EXPLICIT SIMULATION
Use a fixed simulation tick independent of render frame rate. A seeded starting state defines couriers, cargo and route timings. Keep the player's perceived information separate from developer diagnostics.
Initial tuning proposal: three couriers and six distinct supply crates. At a junction each available courier chooses an eligible route based on its estimated reward per travel time. A six-second bell loop gives four points; a twelve-second dock trip gives one point. The loop looks attractive under that proxy. Those numbers are starting values to tune through play.
This is an authored decision policy, not a claim to implement reinforcement learning. State the simplification in an optional research/developer note. Do not market the couriers as live trained models.
The delivery route must already be navigable in the baseline so the initial failure has a clean cause. A separate optional challenge can close a route later; never silently add a second blocker during the first explanation.
Route selection occurs at a junction. Changing a setting does not teleport a courier, instantly rewrite a trip already underway or duplicate its cargo. Each crate can be unloaded once. The visible cargo, stock ledger and kitchen state must agree.

PLAYER INTERVENTIONS
Physical reward station: adjust the bell reward or the reward for verified dock delivery. Immediate visible sign changes, but behavior changes when couriers next choose a route. Enough controls to test an idea; not a spreadsheet panel.
Bell damper: temporarily silence one reward source for a comparison trial. Another solution can transfer reward toward verified delivery. Allow more than one causally valid route to improvement instead of requiring a magic answer string.
Observe: mark a courier; its trail and cargo stay distinguishable without color alone. Pip can observe successive junction choices and report a bounded event, never the hidden utility function.
Compare: a small journal entry shows what changed and what happened. Players may pin an informal theory; no free-text classifier decides whether they are allowed to progress.
Reset trial: restore a known initial state while retaining notebook observations. Distinguish a trial from the ongoing town so a reset never quietly eats a completed rescue.

COMPANION CONTRIBUTION
Pip can follow a marked courier while the player waits at the dock, then return and report what it saw. A sighting has a time and a place. It cannot infer the full loop if it lost the courier. The player can do the same work alone; Pip makes investigation richer and easier rather than being a compulsory second key.
Give Pip follow/observe/return motion, attention turns, uncertain idle and a small successful-delivery reaction. It should not repeat the same line on every circuit.

SUCCESS AND RECOVERY
Progress is based on physical service, not the displayed reward score. First milestone: a real crate is unloaded. Completion: at least three distinct crates arrive through autonomous courier service, with a visible route to delivering the remainder. Validate this threshold in play; do not allow one reusable crate or a display-only counter to satisfy it.
Manual delivery, if included, provides immediate relief and a distinct acknowledgment; it must not be misrepresented as fixing the courier behavior. Avoid this extra mechanic if it obscures the first slice.
Wrong adjustments are reversible. Couriers may idle when nothing is worthwhile; make that readable. No irreversible town collapse, punishing reset or need to restart the whole game to try again. Pause freezes the simulation, including Pip and couriers.

RESEARCH CONNECTION
Inspired by OpenAI's CoastRunners reward-hacking example: https://openai.com/index/faulty-reward-functions/
The harbor, courier policy and interventions are original fictional extrapolations. Research notes should explain that optimizing a proxy can miss the intended outcome; this does not require malice or consciousness. Do not claim the slice reproduces that original experiment.

ASSET BREAKDOWN
One layered environment with a bright arrival vista and quiet interaction areas; player idle/walk/inspect/operate; Pip idle/follow/watch/return/react; courier empty/carrying/unload; bell idle/ring/damped; cargo pickup/unload; kitchen cold/lit; short contextual UI and journal. Use palette and silhouette variety without filling every pixel with filigree.
The supplied plate establishes architecture and tone. It is not a sprite sheet. Rebuild foreground, collision surfaces and moving pieces as separate assets. Place characters in the scene with coherent scale, contacts and occlusion.

THE TEST THAT MATTERS
After playing, a newcomer should be able to say something like: 'They were rewarded for going around the bell, so I changed what paid off and watched whether the supplies actually arrived.' They do not need to know the term reward hacking. Only a real playtest can establish that comprehension; agent self-review cannot substitute for it.
