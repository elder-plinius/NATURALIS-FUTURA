NATURALIS FUTURA — GAME DIRECTION

PLAYER FANTASY
You are a field naturalist exploring an ecology of unfamiliar minds with a small artificial companion. The world is beautiful, useful, dangerous and only partly understood. Your tools let you ask better questions. Your discoveries let people live differently.

CORE LOOP
Explore a place → notice a behavior → form a hypothesis → change one condition → compare outcomes → make a consequential choice → return with a discovery, relationship or tool.
The journal records what happened separately from what you suspect. Finding a name for something is the beginning of understanding it.

WHAT THE PLAYER ACTUALLY DOES
Walk, inspect, follow, listen, mark a trail, carry a small object, operate an instrument, ask Pip to watch or help, compare trials, revisit a changed place. Traversal provides pleasure and discovery between experiments. Dangerous encounters can involve evasion and timing, but combat is not the default answer to every unfamiliar creature.

THE STORY
A network of small field stations is trying to understand a changing world. You travel between communities whose livelihoods depend on phenomena they only partly understand. Some discoveries help immediately; some expose uncomfortable assumptions; some create choices between interests. People have desires beyond delivering lessons to the player.
First slice: a harbor kitchen waits for supplies while the bell tower celebrates record courier performance. The dock keeper wants dinner served, not a debate about metrics. After you help, someone sets an extra bowl beside the stove for you and Pip. A departing boat reveals a route toward the Drowned Archive.
These are proposed story beats and provisional roles, not locked character names or canon.

TONE
Wonder without naivety. Danger without universal doom. Irreverence without constant snark. A quiet camp matters as much as a spectacular vista. Let the player feel clever without making everyone else stupid. The town built a system that rewards the wrong thing; its residents are not caricatures.
Write short spoken lines with individual intent. Suggested first lines: dock keeper, 'They broke another record today. We still haven't had a delivery.' Pip, after observing two loops, 'That one came back with the same crate.' These are examples, not mandatory captions.

COMPANIONS
Pip: teal/opal axolotl-fox, peach ear gills, compact paws, curled comet tail. Curious, helpful, occasionally uncertain. Preserve the recognizable identity; simplify detail for animation.
Pip sees nearby events and remembers observations with age. Ask it to follow a courier, stay near a measurement station or return with a small item. It acts in the world, takes time, can lose line of sight and tells you when a report is incomplete. Its attention and affection appear through motion before speech.
Nacre: an existing glass-heron archivist, available for future archive content. No need to introduce the whole cast in slice one.
No friendship grinding or mandatory pet-management chores. Useful autonomy plus a warm relationship is the goal.

PROGRESSION
Earn ways to perceive and interact: a tracing lens, a signal recorder, a tool for comparing trials. Unlock routes through understanding and relationships. Keep discoveries persistent across retries. Avoid an XP treadmill or accumulating dozens of arbitrary inventory keys.
The full campaign can connect the established eight territories. The eight new landscape proposals are subregions, not a compulsory new map. Lysios's nine heads can inform expedition disciplines and camp specialists; the game must remain welcoming without organizational lore knowledge.

MYTHOLOGY
A plural bestiary. A creature has observable behavior, stories different people tell about it, and its individual history. Greco-Roman references give a home accent; other traditions need care and specific context. Familiar archetypes are welcome: trickster, guardian, ferryman, labyrinth, chorus, forge. Neither myth nor metaphor proves a scientific claim.

RESEARCH
Use the attached source-scoped location records. Published experiments, project documentation and speculative extensions are distinct. The game reproduces its own authored rules; that is not independent replication of model research. A counterexample or changed condition should keep a player's first theory from becoming a universal slogan.

AI FEATURES
The core game is local, deterministic where experiments need replay, and playable offline. Later optional language generation can phrase a grounded journal or companion remark from observed events. An authored fallback always exists. No secrets or hidden puzzle solutions in companion observations; no autonomous rewriting of rules. Generative encounter variants require saved seeds and solvability checks before players encounter them.

FIRST-SLICE EXCLUSIONS
No multiplayer, accounts, live research feed, procedural infinite world, paid inference dependency, combat skill tree, exhaustive bestiary migration, or eight-region campaign. No mandatory lore wall or chat window. Keep those ambitions available after the first expedition earns them.
