## What to take from this package

Open all four numbered concept plates before making aesthetic decisions. Read `README.md`, `GAME-DIRECTION.txt`, `FIRST-EXPEDITION.txt`, and `reference/ART-DIRECTION.txt`. The research catalog supplies eight place/behavior/puzzle proposals with primary sources, counterexamples and limits. Use it as a worldbuilding resource, not a command to implement eight regions now.

The existing Gilded Circuit code proves a small causal loop. Three couriers carry unique cargo. Bell points and real deliveries are separate. Interventions change future choices; comparison trials restore the original town. Pip reports witnessed events. Supplies arriving warm a kitchen and open the ferry. Preserve those properties if you reuse it.

What matters is that the player discovers the contradiction through play, tests a theory, and sees somebody benefit. The exact town name, UI, route, camera, engine and module layout are adjustable. The concept illustrations likewise show intent, not literal collision maps.

## Integrate with judgment

1. **Show the baseline.** Run your existing version. Record what it already does well, unresolved defects, saves and uncommitted work. Preserve the authored atlas, citations, creature content and useful companion systems even if their presentation changes.
2. **Choose the best playable foundation.** Keep your renderer, traversal, audio or companion work when stronger. Port the new encounter into it, or retain the PR's foundations if that is the better route. An engine switch needs a demonstrated benefit, not a prestige argument. Check overlapping arrival-screen/global-style changes before any cherry-pick.
3. **Make one art-and-motion proof.** A short playable stretch with foreground framing, middle-distance paths, distant architecture, material texture, selective light, one animated explorer, Pip and one convincing organism. Movement must remain readable against the detail. Establish the real production method before expanding the map.
4. **Complete the first expedition.** Connect arrival, observation, experiment, consequence and departure. Let geography and inhabitants carry exposition. Tune the route for curiosity, not just walking right between labeled stations.
5. **Review the whole experience.** Compare with the plates at gameplay scale. Play a wrong attempt and recovery. Check cargo, observations, saves, input, timing and performance after visual changes. Keep what is stronger, not simply newer.

## Make the world alive

The explorer looks, balances, probes and writes with intention. Feet contact surfaces. A courier anticipates a turn and visibly lowers its cargo before releasing it. Pip notices something before speaking, chooses a vantage point, hesitates when it loses the trail, and returns with an observation it actually made. The kitchen's change is an event: arrival, unloading, response, warmth, an extra bowl. Vary inhabitants rather than recoloring the player three times.

Give the Gilded Circuit its impossible aqueduct and recognizable bell tower. Let the route loop legibly around the landmark; make the unattended supplies visible from a useful vantage. Use sound and repeated action to invite investigation. A secondary path, viewing perch or small optional discovery should reward exploration without adding mandatory chores.

The broader world can be threatening without turning every creature into an enemy. A guardian may protect a nursery. A song may coordinate a community in one context and mislead it in another. A strange signal may be only noise. Let conditions change the meaning of what the player sees.

## Art production choices you can own

Use layered paintings, authored vectors, cutout rigs, sprite animation, a suitable 2D/3D combination, or an equivalent approach that achieves the result on the target browser. Fine detail can be baked and cached. Separate contact surfaces, occlusion and animated props from scenery. Budget textures and particles; keep reduced effects and motion options. Neither flat polygons everywhere nor a moving camera over one beautiful painting is an adequate substitute for an inhabited game.

Protect Pip's identity but simplify intelligently. Keep teal/opal, peach gills, small paws and comet tail. Reserve conspicuous glow for attention or phenomena. Keep UI quiet enough to let the place speak; the research explanation belongs after the player has something to explain.

## Future scope, once the first expedition earns it

The Prism Depths uses interference; the Drowned Archive uses position-sensitive retrieval; Whisperroot Delta uses confused authority; the Second-Spring Orchard uses conditioned triggers. Tongue-Stone Quarry, Hidden Tide and Faultline Forge connect to language, concealed signals and interventions. Each needs an observable rule, a controllable test, a comparison and a reason someone cares.

Optional AI can phrase a journal from recorded observations or provide bounded variations with reproducible seeds and solvability checks. It must not invent evidence, reveal hidden puzzle state, change rules silently, or become a prerequisite for basic movement and experiments.

Be ambitious about the experience and pragmatic about the implementation. We want a place to explore with a friend, with the science embedded in what happens there.
